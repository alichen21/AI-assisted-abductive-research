# Literature coverage and recency

Apply these rules whenever this skill conducts a search, including a scoped
search for existing theoretical explanations. Respect an explicitly historical
question, a supplied-paper-only task, or the researcher's stated time/depth
limit; record the chosen scope instead of silently expanding it.

## Search through the actual search date

- Record the actual search date and exact date boundaries. Unless the task is
  historical, search through that date. Default recent coverage is the rolling
  last five years, with a separate check of the last two years, including the
  current year to date. Compute the dates at execution time; do not inherit
  fixed years from examples, previous reviews, or model knowledge.
- Keep an unrestricted-date search for foundations. In a distinct recent pass,
  apply publication-date filters and newest-first ordering where supported.
  Check publisher online-first/early-access articles and relevant working-paper
  or preprint sources when eligible. Mark publication status and link versions;
  a preprint and its journal article are not two independent studies.
- Verify publication dates against the bibliographic or publisher record. A
  webpage update date, search-engine freshness label, repository upload, or
  revised preprint date is not necessarily a new publication or finding.
- Use available scholarly search tools, connectors, database interfaces/APIs,
  or web search. The optional Parallel CLI is not a prerequisite. Search live
  sources; remembered references may seed a search but cannot establish current
  coverage. If access fails, disclose the failed source and use an accessible
  alternative. Never describe an unexecuted query as a completed search.

## Cover explanations as well as familiar labels

Build a small query matrix from the supplied question, candidate lens, focal
phenomenon, and necessary context. Search relevant combinations, rather than
requiring all concepts in every query:

| Search purpose | What to seek |
| --- | --- |
| Foundations | Original theoretical arguments and major developments, without a date cutoff |
| Recent developments | Extensions, refinements, tests, boundary conditions, and new mechanisms in the recent windows |
| Phenomenon and adjacent explanations | Alternative terminology and competing accounts, beyond the named lens |
| Disagreement and limits | Contradictory findings, critiques, negative results, and scope limits |

For a substantive review, use at least two complementary scholarly discovery
sources appropriate to the discipline, or document the access/scope limitation.
Multiple queries on one engine do not constitute multiple sources. In social
science, organization, and management topics, choose relevant multidisciplinary
indexes, journal/publisher searches, and working-paper repositories rather than
defaulting to biomedical databases. Sources and query variants should add
coverage; a fixed database count alone does not establish completeness.

Follow references backward and citations forward from useful foundational AND
recent papers or reviews. Log seed papers and how far chaining was inspected.
Search by mechanisms, synonyms, and related constructs when the exact label is
sparse. Do not use citation counts, prestige, familiarity, or free full-text
availability as relevance criteria. Record inaccessible eligible reports and
limit claims to the text actually retrieved.

In a theory-first handoff, build queries from the caller's permitted background;
this protocol does not authorize reading case outcomes to select a theory or
change any condition's analysis sequence.

## Breadth and when to stop

For a normal substantive, scoped theory search, plan to screen roughly 30–50
unique candidate records and work toward roughly 15–25 relevant publications
when the literature supports it. These are adjustable planning targets, not
eligibility quotas, caps, or evidence of completeness. A quick lookup may need
much less; a broad or systematic review may need much more. Retain all eligible
records in a systematic review. Report the actual numbers, even if small.

Do not stop at a handful of familiar classics or the first ranked results page.
Inspect further result pages/batches when available and record truncation or
inspection limits. If the pool is small, dominated by classics, or missing a
relevant search purpose, broaden terminology, relax unnecessary conjunctions,
check another source, and inspect recent forward citations before concluding
that little relevant work exists. Keep off-topic records excluded.

Stop a scoped search when the relevant search purposes and recent windows have
been checked and an additional targeted expansion adds little material to the
explanations, disputes, or scope conditions, or when an explicit time/access
limit is reached. Record which expansion was tried and what it added; distinguish
coverage-based stopping from a limited or incomplete search. Do not claim
exhaustiveness or theoretical novelty from low yield. There is no required
recent-paper percentage: report a sparse recent literature honestly.

## Make coverage inspectable

Keep the record in the caller's existing literature artifact or the review's
methods/appendix, with saved search outputs where available. Include:

- Search date, scope, exact recent windows, and source coverage/access limits.
- Per search: source/tool, exact query, filters/order, run date, returned total
  if actually supplied, records inspected/exported, paging or ranking limits,
  and saved-output reference. Unknown totals remain unknown.
- Actual retrieved and deduplicated candidate counts, screening decisions and
  reasons, included publications, and full-text versus abstract-only access.
  Keep records, reports, and studies distinct when reporting systematic counts.
- Included-paper counts in the five-year and nested two-year windows, oldest
  and newest publication dates, and the explanations or disputes represented.
  The two recent counts overlap; do not add them together as disjoint groups.
- A source table: title/authors/year, DOI or stable source URL, publication
  status, search provenance, relevance/role, and retrieved-text level. For each
  substantive theoretical claim, preserve its source location and distinguish
  the author's argument from the analyst's synthesis.
- Recent synthesis: what recent work extends, challenges, or leaves unchanged
  in the foundational account, with actual source support. Do not merely append
  new titles to an otherwise unchanged summary.
- Stopping rationale, expansions performed, unresolved coverage gaps, and how
  those gaps constrain the review's conclusions.

Do not fabricate records, search counts, recent papers, full-text access, or
mechanisms to meet a planning target. Loading this file is not search execution.
