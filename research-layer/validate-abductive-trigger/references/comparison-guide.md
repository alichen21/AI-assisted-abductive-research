# Comparison and evidence guide

Read as needed for multiple theories, typologies, or stages. Use relevant sections; filling tables is not equivalent to analysis.

Internally link Comparison IDs to Explanation, Implication, source locators/Evidence IDs and input version (existing Code IDs optional). Researcher prose states the actual observations, theoretical claims, and reasoning. Full IDs belong in the appendix/index, with short review labels mapped to revisions. See [researcher report writing](report-writing.md).

## Enter comparison through arguments and case predictions

Save literature arguments and case predictions under the [theory-first protocol](theory-first.md), then inspect both evidence sides with the [prediction-level template](../templates/theory-observation-comparison.md). Candidate development below follows this comparison; it does not authorize scanning data first for matching theories.

| Abstract argument from literature | Case prediction and derivation |
|---|---|

| Saved case prediction | Supporting evidence | Counter-evidence/unresolved | Preliminary assessment and reasons |
|---|---|---|---|

Account for every prediction, not only those with matching evidence. State substantive content in prose and preserve full IDs/versions in the index. Distinguish departures under applicable conditions from phenomena for which theory gives no expectation. The latter are neither automatic counterexamples nor automatic novelty. Observation—Coverage—Preliminary Novelty may summarize results but does not replace these checks.

## Develop candidates within Steps 4–5

Establish what theory actually explains, then seek evidenced residual demands. Ruling out a faulty formulation should help build a better question rather than merely label it unoriginal. This is an obligation within the current episode, not another task requiring a manual launch.

| Current judgment/situation | What to examine next | Possible candidate and remaining checks |
|---|---|---|
| An outcome/relationship is covered | Does the existing mechanism actually explain the process, sequence, and actions producing it? | Evidence of a different process may support a mechanism lead; an unrecorded mechanism is only an evidence gap |
| A familiar concept names an action | Does the explanation account for this choice, timing, and outcome difference, or merely rename behavior? | Reformulate concept membership as a checkable relationship/process question; verify prior discussion |
| The case is broadly compatible | Why do similar starts have different follow-through, similar actions receive different responses, or the same actor change? | Existing contrasts may reveal additional demands involving conditions, mechanisms, or processes; difference alone does not establish discrepancy |
| Observations cross stages, levels, or participants | What does theory explain within each part, and what remains at their connection? | Examine a transition/connection candidate; new technology or being outside the original sample alone is insufficient |
| Existing explanations dissolve the initial hunch | Can other verifiable raw or existing observations support a more precise question that distinguishes rivals? | Record reformulation without stopping at rejection or guaranteeing novelty |
| Unresolved | Is uncertainty caused by missing data, weak derivation, competing explanations, or an evidenced residual demand? | Recheck available evidence/extraction or bounded literature where possible; missing data alone is not novelty |

Do not run every check mechanically or generate a fixed number of candidates. Prioritize evidential strength, examinability, and potential explanatory significance. Adjacent checks serve the bounded primary-lens question. Hand decisions about independent new-lens episodes, raw data, or deep L2 work to the researcher.

### A proportionate candidate threshold

- A candidate needs concrete observations/contrasts, an identified existing explanation and residual demand, and completed relevant/adjacent literature checks. State what further evidence would weaken or overturn it.
- Candidate Novelty need not supply a complete alternative theory. Candidate Discrepancy need not complete causal identification, but must identify scope conditions and an evidenced departure.
- Remaining rivals do not automatically exclude a candidate. Explain what they cover and why examination remains worthwhile. If an available explanation adequately covers the demand, rule out or reformulate rather than keep repackaging it.
- Specify the object of Covered: a compatible pattern does not identify its mechanism. Do not treat every detail as a gap or a broad conceptual label as complete explanation.
- Distinguish leads, candidate Findings meeting the current retention threshold, and later researcher-confirmed judgments. Leads can live in search records; do not add an Assessment Type or automatically promote them to TRED.

### Preserve candidate development and disposition

Save actual work in candidate-search.md with this structure or equivalent prose:

| Observation/contrast | Existing explanatory coverage | Residual demand and candidate formulation | Supporting/contrary evidence and adjacent checks | Disposition and rationale | Most discriminating next check |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Disposition may describe retention, reformulation, existing coverage, data gap only, or blocked checking. These are search descriptions, not new Finding categories. Preserve original attempts and reasons, with theory/evidence links. The report presents substantive progress without treating every lead as a discovery.

Only after bounded active checking can no retained trigger count as a complete episode. If checking could not occur, mark partial/blocked. Genuine zero results are valid; repeatedly declaring non-novelty without advancing the search is insufficient.

## Internal detailed comparison and provenance

| Theory/source | Abstract explanation/classification and scope | Case-level construct mapping | Expected observations under stated conditions | Actual observations and source locators (existing codes optional) | Judgment, rivals, next step |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Preserve paths from literature to expectation and from original record to empirical mapping. Acknowledge prior case knowledge in retrospective comparisons.

Expert praise may indicate an audience's evaluation of one performance attribute, not regulatory permission, purchases, or general legitimacy. Signatures, equipment investment, and repeated use need not form one progressive scale. Describe commitment content, conditions, and implementation.

## Typology comparison

| Dimensions/type definitions | Unit/period | Corresponding features | Membership/hybrid status | Unmatched features | Classification/explanatory implications |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Do not assume mutually exclusive, exhaustive typologies. Check allowed hybrids, deviations from ideal types, and transitions before treating them as gaps.

## Accumulation across stages

| Stage and technical/business trajectory | Recognition and its specific object | Commitments and actual participation | Carryover, recombination, or discontinuation | How new support is sought | Observable outcomes/sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Distinguish continuity of capability, continued participation by the same actor, renewed display of prior projects, and acceptance of earlier recognition by a new audience. The first three do not independently prove the fourth. Participant turnover does not automatically mean network abandonment.

## Focused evidence needs

| Explanations to distinguish | Existing leads | Priority episodes/materials | Contrasting expectations under A/B | What remains supportable if unavailable |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Prioritize differing follow-through after similar announcements, a partner's changes over stages, or audience responses to similar actions. Internal records, interviews, and complete technical versions are not default prerequisites. Announcements, contracts, standards, project status, withdrawal, and delivery can supply contrasts. Bound conclusions by observability.

## Three illustrative judgments

These illustrate reasoning, not actual study findings.

**Same pattern, open mechanism.** A theory explains periphery-to-center movement through adaptation to mainstream rules. Similar movement accompanied by jointly changed evaluative rules can suggest another process. Establish the timing and connection to movement, and check whether institutional work or adjacent explanations already cover it before naming a new mechanism.

**Typology mismatch, no new type yet.** Features of two types require checks of consistent units, permitted hybrids, and improperly combined stages. Retain a candidate only if a stable, explanatorily meaningful combination remains uncovered.

**Similar announcements, different implementation.** Some signed collaborations deliver while others remain announcements. Compare prior relationships, commitment conditions, and follow-up. Nondelivery does not directly establish lost legitimacy; signatures do not establish received funds.

## Compact candidate formulation

Use as an argument-completeness aid, not a rigid sentence:

“Under [conditions], existing research explains [relationship/process] through [explanation/mechanism/classification]. Here, [sourced observations] create [specific additional explanatory demand]. [Rival] still covers part of the evidence. Within [search/evidence boundaries], retain [candidate discrepancy/novelty/scope issue/unresolved] and next compare [discriminating observations].”
