# Researcher-facing report writing

assessment.md explains this episode's grounded judgments. Full derivations, checks of both evidence sides for every prediction, and candidate development remain in their respective analysis files. Select enough content for readers to understand and evaluate the judgments without reprinting the research process. Shorter presentation does not reduce research steps or evidence checks.

## Make the judgment concrete

Give each focal finding an informative title and one or two short paragraphs, usually covering:

- **Object and conclusion.** Identify who did what under which conditions, or the relationship between specific events. State Covered, candidate, scope issue, or unresolved. “A complex tension exists” or a question beginning “How…” is insufficient.
- **Decisive comparison.** Which publication explains or predicts what, under which conditions? Which sourced observation fits, departs from, or lies beyond that explanatory object? Cite author/year and empirical locators beside the claims; distinguish theoretical claims, observations, and analyst inference.
- **Why the judgment stops here.** State the contrary material, checked rival, or unverified condition that most affects the conclusion, explaining its consequence. If no contrary evidence was found, state the actual inspection scope rather than inventing a counterexample. Mark an unchecked rival as unchecked, not eliminated.
- **If disputed, what would change the judgment?** Identify a concrete distinction and observable evidence: for example, who stopped which proposal, when, and for what reason. “Explore dynamic mechanisms further” is insufficient. A suggested check does not authorize new research.

Connect these naturally; four subheadings or four separate paragraphs are unnecessary. Links enable verification, but the prose must supply the essential fact and argument rather than require readers to reconstruct them from files.

## Specify the additional explanatory demand

“Personal commitment becomes collective action,” “dynamic negotiation of identities,” and “boundary reconstruction” are summaries. Ground them in actors, actions, sequence, or relationships: what do the examined accounts explain, what remains unexplained, and why is this an explanatory gap rather than missing information?

A more detailed question is not automatically more theoretically novel. Adding roles, stages, or context labels without identifying a substantive remainder relative to an existing account leaves a question for investigation. Where evidence is insufficient, say Unresolved rather than presenting a possible mechanism as a retained theoretical discovery.

- **Covered:** Identify the observation and account, with unidentified mechanisms or scope limits. Behavioral fit does not validate the theory.
- **Candidate Discrepancy:** State the original prediction, evidence that conditions hold, and sourced departure. Theoretical silence is not a negative prediction.
- **Candidate Novelty:** State the evidence-based additional explanatory object and why examined relevant/rival explanations remain insufficient. Bound the claim to the lens or documented broader search.
- **Scope Issue/Unresolved:** Identify the specific applicability condition, evidence link, or rival distinction not established, and which judgment that prevents. Avoid generic “more data needed.”

Integrate Who cares/Why/How much into a sentence or two: which researchers would need to reconsider which relationship, mechanism, or scope condition if the candidate holds, and how far the current evidence goes. Do not fill separate boxes with importance, complexity, or promise; do not declare a contribution or conduct L3 scoring.

## Default to a short report

1. Open with two or three sentences giving the result and decisive grounds. Include lens, access, or prior-exposure qualifications where they affect interpretation.
2. Develop the focal judgments needing researcher evaluation, usually about 90–150 words each, adjusted as necessary. Account for other Findings in a reasoned sentence each with individual feedback labels; do not repeatedly explain the same fact.
3. Use a short paragraph for what candidate search actually ruled out/reformulated and the most discriminating next check. With no retained candidates, report actual checks and exclusion reasons rather than padding discoveries or future-work lists.
4. End with concise source/record links and review-label-to-Finding/Revision mappings. Link existing bibliographies, all predictions, material lists, and technical logs rather than reprinting them.

About 500–750 words of main text is a useful default, excluding provenance mappings. Simpler results can be shorter; additional important findings or consequential counterevidence can justify more. Length is not a quota and must not remove evidence that changes a conclusion, important limitations, or a Finding's disposition.

Choose short prose or one useful comparison table. Explain content once rather than stacking theory, evidence, observation-summary tables and repeated discussions. Preserve complete checks for every prediction in theory-observation-comparison.md; assessment need not reproduce every row. The body still presents the theory–expectation–evidence comparison decisive for the conclusions.

Mark unanticipated observations as exploratory; never invent prior predictions. Offer a one-sentence candidate research question only when the judgments support that narrowing, not as a routine polished deliverable or a Research State update.

## Wording and provenance

- Label empirical sources by recognizable title/date and page, paragraph, or timestamp; identify theoretical claims by author/year and source location. Several quotations from one interview are not independent sources.
- Mark abstract-only access or prior reading notes beside affected claims. Do not reconstruct unknown mechanisms, quotations, or metadata. A bibliography alone is not a theoretical argument.
- Ground abstractions such as transformation, coordination, reconstruction, and tension in actors, actions, and relationships. Attach qualifications to the exact claim they limit instead of repeatedly hedging every sentence.
- Match the bound finding revision. Keep long UUIDs, JSON keys, and ID chains out of the body. Short labels must resolve to all Finding/Revision records and analysis artifacts; keep Review Status separate from Assessment Type.
- Save presentation-only edits as separate reading versions without overwriting old reports, Findings, or committed History or inventing theoretical revisions.

Other analysis artifacts retain the detail their tasks require: literature-review organizes accounts/disputes, theoretical-explanations preserves mechanisms/conditions, and comparison checks every prediction against both evidence sides. The assessment length guidance does not limit those records.

## Edit before delivery

For each central judgment, identify the concrete fact and theoretical claim that determine it, the strongest rival or contrary evidence, and why these support this classification. If only terminology, a question, or evaluative adjectives remain, add grounds or narrow the conclusion to unresolved.

Then try deleting each sentence: does the reader lose a fact, inference, judgment boundary, or useful next step? If not, delete it. Remove evaluations reusable after merely changing the case name, repeated method declarations, synonymous conclusions, and internal acceptance-check text. Narrow unverifiable claims rather than smoothing them over.
