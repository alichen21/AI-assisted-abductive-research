# News Analysis: What Lessons Does ZX Moto's Breakthrough Victory Offer?

> Source: [Xinhua News Agency](https://www.news.cn/20260401/c6ec9cee57f54f8e8211b8e843e7cd88/c.html)

## Lessons from ZX Moto's Breakthrough: Stay Passionate, Stay Focused, Move Forward

Zhang Xue

Twenty years ago, Zhang Xue was a teenager who chased a vehicle through the rain for three hours. Today, Chongqing Zhangxue Motorcycle Industry Co., Ltd., the company he founded, has made Chinese motorcycles an overnight sensation.

At the recently concluded Portuguese round of the Superbike World Championship (WSBK), the independently manufactured Chinese ZX Moto won both WorldSSP races. The achievement broke a decades-long monopoly by European, American, and Japanese brands and marked a historic WSBK breakthrough for a Chinese motorcycle manufacturer.

People like this underdog success story, but perhaps they like even more the light that has always shone in Zhang's eyes.

On interview videos, viewers' on-screen comments call him "pure," "a child at heart," "full of passion," and "remarkable." That sincerity and passion took him beyond his usual circle and touched millions.

Countless people discuss and applaud Zhang, likely celebrating exactly this quality: "light in his eyes, love in his heart, and focus on moving forward."

An interviewer asked him: "Why are you still so uncomplicated today? You are nearly forty, yet you have not become a complicated person."

His answer was characteristically Zhang: "If I did not do this, I would feel I was getting a poor deal out of life. We only have a few short decades. Should we not enjoy them properly and fully?"

His goals come from stable values and powerful internal motivation, rather than outside expectations: taking what he believes in to the limit and giving everything for his own life.

As some people grow older, they gradually understand that passion is also a form of long-term commitment that can be adjusted, and that focus is what takes you farther.

Passion can be fine-tuned. Zhang loved motorcycles from childhood, but originally wanted to race, rather than build them. Repairing motorcycles and appearing on television were indirect routes toward that goal. When he realized he might never become the best racer, he felt disappointed but soon told himself that a bike he built winning was the same as winning himself.

Focus can overcome time. Born in 1987 and raised by his grandmother, Zhang began living independently at fourteen. Everything depended on his own efforts, and he taught himself both repair and riding skills.

He began practicing in 2004, spending more than 200 days out of each 365 riding on wasteland, vowing to become the best motorcycle racer.

In 2006, nineteen-year-old Zhang ran a motorcycle repair shop in his hometown of Mayang, Huaihua, Hunan. Sometimes he had only RMB 200–300 in his pocket. He found ways to follow Hunan Satellite TV journalists working locally, chasing their vehicle for three hours in the rain. He hoped television exposure would get him into a team and into racing.

After the program aired, a team really did call. In April 2007, he went to Jiangsu to train on his own. In two years, he spent all his savings of tens of thousands of yuan and eventually had to yield briefly to reality.

In 2013, he traveled alone to Chongqing to start a business. Knowing nobody there, he accumulated his first capital by posting modification work on motorcycle forums and selling bikes.

His choice of Chongqing was well grounded.

In 1979, China's first civilian motorcycle, the Jialing CJ50, rolled off a Chongqing production line. Private companies including Lifan, Zongshen, and Loncin subsequently rose. In 2025, Chongqing produced 7.857 million motorcycles, the most of any Chinese city and 35.5% of national output. It exported 6.109 million, or 77.8% of its output. Five of China's ten leading motorcycle exporters were Chongqing companies. The city has 43 vehicle manufacturers and more than 400 supporting enterprises above the designated size, with over 80% local sourcing for fuel-powered motorcycles.

In 2017, Zhang and partners founded Kove. After leaving Kove, he established ZX Moto in Chongqing in April 2024, focusing on core engine development and the motorcycle products he loves.

The "10,000-hour rule" says that, whatever you do, persisting for 10,000 hours will generally make you an expert in that field. Zhang once publicly assembled a motorcycle engine blindfolded—undoubtedly a result of focus.

To remain "more focused," he also carefully designed ZX Moto's ownership structure. Even after its latest funding round, he and Chongqing Zhangxue Partners Enterprise Management Partnership (Limited Partnership) together held more than 80%.

In 2026, the three-cylinder 820RR supersport went on sale. This road-going production bike, modified for track use, went straight into the Superbike World Championship. With an independently developed Chinese engine, key components, and vehicle setup, it took two wins.

At the brand's partner conference in early 2026, Zhang said its 2025 total output value reached RMB 750 million. R&D spending was RMB 69.58 million, equivalent to 9.33% of sales, and its loss for the period was RMB 22.78 million. He also disclosed that ZX Moto completed a Series A financing round in January 2026, expecting RMB 90 million in investment at a post-money valuation of RMB 1.09 billion.

One manufacturing-industry participant sees ZX Moto's breakthrough as a microcosm of China's advance into global high-end manufacturing, representing the accelerating move upmarket in Chinese industry. Behind it lie the concentration and effort of countless manufacturing workers, day after day and year after year. (Reporter: Xia Zihang)
