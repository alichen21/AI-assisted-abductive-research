# Zhang Xue's Former Employer Has Its IPO Application Accepted: How Far Is ZX Moto from Listing?

> Source: [Beijing Daily](https://xinwen.bjd.com.cn/content/s6acd7f1ce4b039a8e2f23b4f.html)

2026-10-13 08:45

After ZX Moto secured another funding round, Zhang Xue's former employer Apollo also moved closer to the capital market. Zhejiang Apollo Sports Technology Co., Ltd.'s application for an initial public offering on the Beijing Stock Exchange was recently accepted. The history between its chairwoman and joint actual controller Ying Er and Zhang connects the two motorcycle businesses, drawing attention to the capital-market development of this relatively traditional manufacturing industry.

Zhang's former employer pursues an IPO

According to local Zhejiang media, Apollo's connection with Zhang began in 2009. At twenty-two, he wrote a job-application letter in pencil, hoping to work at the motorcycle company in Wuyi, Zhejiang. His academic record and résumé were unremarkable, but his passion for repairs and racing moved Ying, who gave him a chance to start as a mechanic.

At Apollo, he progressed from repairs and testing to product development. Ying supported his idea to develop off-road motorcycles. In 2013, he left for Chongqing with thirty sets of components and RMB 20,000 to start a business, later co-founding Kove and establishing ZX Moto in 2024.

Years later, internet users resurfaced a video of Zhang congratulating Apollo on an anniversary, bringing Ying wider recognition. The woman popularly called "the boss's wife" is actually Apollo's chairwoman and principal shareholder. Its prospectus says she directly holds 57%, while her husband Xu Kai holds 38%. Together they directly own 95% and jointly control the company.

While ZX Moto centers on high-performance motorcycle R&D and racing validation, Apollo has long operated overseas, supplying electric two-wheelers, gasoline-powered off-road motorcycles, and all-terrain vehicles for outdoor recreation, competitive sport, and other uses.

Its prospectus shows that from 2023 to 2025, revenue rose from RMB 281 million to RMB 588 million, and net profit attributable to the parent from RMB 41.8434 million to RMB 67.0183 million. Throughout the reporting period, overseas sales accounted for over 97% of principal-business revenue, with products sold mainly in Europe and North America.

Years of operating results laid the foundation for capital-market entry. Apollo selected the Beijing Stock Exchange's first listing standard. Net profit attributable to the parent, using the lower figure before or after nonrecurring items, was RMB 38.3269 million in 2024 and RMB 67.0183 million in 2025. Corresponding weighted average returns on net assets were 18.11% and 24.99%, meeting profitability and return requirements.

Growth in scale brings pressure on profits

While depending on exports, Apollo is electrifying its products. Electric two-wheeler revenue rose from RMB 119 million in 2023 to RMB 277 million in 2025, increasing from 42.78% to 47.69% of principal-business revenue and reaching 54.38% in the first quarter of 2026. Beyond electric off-road motorcycles, it has developed electric riding products for young people, expanding sales through overseas customers' promotion and e-commerce.

Overseas expansion has also involved price concessions. Europe's share of principal-business revenue rose from 56.02% in 2023 to 67.92% in 2025. Apollo disclosed price cuts on some products to deepen customer partnerships and expand in Europe. Increased sales to some lower-margin customers also affected profitability.

Between 2023 and 2025, gross margins on gasoline motorcycles fell from 26.80% to 18.71%, and those on all-terrain vehicles from 22.51% to 16.07%. In the first quarter of 2026, they fell further to 16.79% and 12.45%.

The latest results show a clearer divergence between revenue growth and core profitability. First-half 2026 revenue was RMB 307 million, up 36.78% year-on-year. Net profit attributable to the parent was RMB 27.0399 million, up 14.12%, but net profit excluding nonrecurring items was RMB 24.0307 million, down 14.70%, while operating profit fell 8.33%.

Apollo still relies primarily on ODM, or original design manufacturing, providing design and manufacturing services for clients. In 2025, complete-vehicle ODM generated 61.77% of principal-business revenue, while own-brand sales accounted for 33.62%. The five largest customers contributed 59.39% of revenue.

For this IPO, Apollo plans to invest about RMB 185 million, nearly half the proposed proceeds, in a base with annual capacity of 50,000 electric off-road motorcycles and 170,000 electric-drive systems. Core electric-drive components are currently mainly purchased externally; the project would add in-house motor and controller production. About RMB 118 million would go toward raising off-road vehicle capacity to 300,000 annually and upgrading smart manufacturing. With complete-vehicle capacity utilization at 101.23% in 2025, expansion meets a real need.

The wider industry similarly combines growth with profit pressure. China Chamber of Commerce for Motorcycles figures show sales of 16.3937 million motorcycles in the first eight months of 2026, up 13.23%, and exports of 10.2941 million complete vehicles, up 17.77%. Exports remain an important growth driver. Yet in the first seven months, manufacturers' revenue rose 11.57% while total profits fell 4.41%.

How far is ZX Moto from listing?

As its founder's former employer pursues an IPO, ZX Moto's own listing timetable attracts attention. It received RMB 20 million from Gaoxin Capital in 2024. In January 2026, Zhejiang Venture Capital led a RMB 90 million Series A at a post-money valuation of RMB 1.09 billion. Following its rise to fame, it announced HongShan investment in August. Tianyancha shows that corporate registration changes for the related share transfer were recently completed: Zhang transferred 2.5% to a HongShan investment platform, with the round valuing ZX Moto at RMB 6 billion.

Asked in June about capital-market plans, Zhang replied, "Let things take their course." Zhejiang Venture Capital general manager Cheng Junhua previously said in an interview that, given Zhang's technical accumulation and the team's execution, a listing before 2030 could be anticipated.

Founded in April 2024, ZX Moto is still less than three years old. Under the Measures for the Administration of Registration of Initial Public Offerings of Stocks applicable to the Shanghai and Shenzhen markets, an issuer must have been lawfully established and continuously operating for more than three years. If a limited liability company converts in its entirety into a joint-stock company by converting its original book net assets into shares, its operating history may count from the limited company's establishment.

Financial performance is another issue. At this year's partner conference, Zhang disclosed 2025 R&D spending of RMB 69.58 million and a full-year loss of RMB 22.78 million. A listing route based on profit metrics would require a turnaround and evidence of sustainable profits. Although current ChiNext rules include market-capitalization and revenue standards without a direct profitability requirement, companies must still meet board-positioning and governance requirements and provide audited financial data.

Motorcycle and related powersports-equipment companies have a long history of accessing capital markets. CFMOTO listed on Shanghai's main board in 2017; Taotao Motor and Huayang Racing listed on ChiNext and the Beijing Stock Exchange respectively in 2023. Apollo had already joined the National Equities Exchange and Quotations and signed a listing-advisory agreement before ZX Moto's rise this year. Against that background, ZX Moto has brought fresh attention to the industry.

Gao Chengyuan, head of the Tiaoyuan Influence Research Institute, sees ZX Moto as catalyzing the industry's capital-market development. Its exclusive HongShan investment at about RMB 6 billion post-money challenges investors' stereotype of Chinese motorcycles as low-end and low-profit. Acceptance of former employer Zhejiang Apollo's Beijing Stock Exchange IPO application and Huayang Racing's share-issuance acquisition plans indicate rising investor interest. ZX Moto does not yet meet listing requirements, however. Its demonstration effect lies mainly in financing: private markets are willing to grant higher valuations to motorcycle businesses with technical barriers and brand pricing power.

Technical investment and capital needs also coincide with policy changes. Gao believes adjustments to motorcycle manufacturing entry requirements and the easing of restrictions in some cities help companies strengthen R&D and expand consumer markets. But improving industry conditions do not mean issuance and listing standards will relax because one company becomes popular. Businesses must demonstrate technical barriers and sustainable profitability, rather than relying on enthusiasm for the sector itself.
