# From County-Town Mechanic to the World's Summit: ZX Moto Opens 170 Stores in Nine Months; Hunan-Born Boss Targets RMB 2 Billion in Sales This Year

> Source: [Time Finance](https://www.time-weekly.com/post/328493)

## From County-Town Mechanic to the World's Summit: ZX Moto Opens 170 Stores in Nine Months; Hunan-Born Boss Targets RMB 2 Billion in Sales This Year

> It also has backing from Zhejiang state-owned capital.

Image source: ZX Moto's official WeChat account.

Zhang Xue's dream of becoming a champion has finally come true.

On March 28–29, at Portugal's Portimão circuit, French rider Valentin Debise rode ZX Moto's 820RR-RS to victory in both races of the Superbike World Championship (WSBK). It was the first time a Chinese motorcycle manufacturer had reached the top in this premier WSBK competition, breaking decades of dominance in the class by international brands including Ducati, Yamaha, and Kawasaki.

ZX Moto's thirty-nine-year-old founder Zhang Xue became tearful beside the circuit. Before competing, he had set three-year goals: "Podium in year one, a race win in year two, the championship in year three." They have now been achieved ahead of schedule.

WSBK motorcycles must be derived from production models, generally with more than 2,000 produced. Industry insiders say winning demonstrates that the production bike's ultimate engine performance, frame rigidity, and electronic calibration rank among the world's best.

Just six months earlier, Zhang said in an interview: "I cannot become an outstanding rider myself. But if an outstanding rider takes a racing motorcycle I build and wins, that is really the same as me winning."

From repair apprentice to the creator behind a world champion, Zhang has staged a real-life version of Pegasus on a Chinese racing motorcycle at a world-leading circuit.

The founder has a junior secondary education and was once a county-town mechanic

ZX Moto's slogan is "Extreme Passion." Zhang himself is its truest embodiment.

Born in a remote mountain village in Huaihua, Hunan, in 1987, he was obsessed with motorcycles from childhood. Poverty forced him to take an indirect route toward his dream of racing. After graduating from junior secondary school in 2003, he became a repair apprentice; at seventeen, he opened his own shop.

"Just give me a moment." In a 2006 video from Hunan Satellite TV's Evening News, nineteen-year-old Zhang stopped a journalist and begged for a chance to appear on television. Rejected, he climbed onto his twenty-year-old Honda, bought for a little over RMB 1,000, and followed the crew along the hundred-kilometer mountain road from Huaihua to Mayang.

By Mayang, he was covered in mud. Asked why, he said: "Being on television is not important; getting into a team is." On camera, he tried hard to persuade the crew: "Whether you succeed or fail, if you do not try when you are young, you will regret it when you are old. If you try when you are young, even if you fail when you are old, you will not regret it."

Unfortunately, his immature skills meant he could not demonstrate especially impressive riding on television. But his persistence moved people. In 2007, he joined a professional motorcycle team to learn under a professional rider. He was not a formal team member, so all living and training costs remained his responsibility. In his words, those two years involved "only spending, no income."

Zhang at nineteen. Screenshot from Hunan Satellite TV's Evening News.

In 2009, with his resources exhausted, he left the team. "I could no longer become a really outstanding professional racer. I had to earn money to eat. Whenever I watched a program about athletes on CCTV-5, I cried as I watched." He then spent three years at Zhejiang Apollo Motorcycle Company.

In 2013, carrying RMB 20,000, he went alone to Chongqing, known as the "motorcycle kitchen," to start building bikes.

Initially, he posted daily on automotive forums, slowly building a following, then bought market components and modified bikes himself. Over the following years, he accumulated his first capital by selling assembled motorcycles to enthusiasts—the true startup funds for his venture.

In 2017, he and two partners founded Tibet Everest Kove Industrial Co., Ltd., creating the Kove brand and subsequently introducing major models including the 450RR and 500X. Its first model launched in August 2018, selling around 800 in year one and 3,000 the next. "Then it was 20,000, 30,000," Zhang recalled in a 2025 interview.

From the outset, he insisted on independent engine development to avoid critical dependencies. For a startup, however, investment of tens of millions was too risky for his partners. He borrowed from the company in his own name and pushed engine development himself, ultimately spending RMB 30 million to create an independently developed large-displacement engine suitable for mass production.

In March 2024, he left the company he had helped build. He said in an interview that his insistence on heavy engine R&D investment led to serious disagreement with partners. He had already been removed as general manager before leaving. After one final failed attempt to secure their investment, he painfully chose to depart.

That April, Chongqing Zhangxue Motorcycle Industry Co., Ltd.—today's ZX Moto—was born. Why that name? "It feels like putting myself on the line," he said.

ZX Moto sales boom; deliveries take three months

Its impressive international racing performance has also energized consumer sales.

"There have been so many inquiries these two days. We have already booked twenty bikes," a ZX Moto store manager in one city told Time Finance on March 30. Even before the victories, that store was doing well: "We consistently sold forty to fifty a month."

The brand still has few models. Its official website lists just the 500RR, 820RR, and 500F, priced between RMB 29,900 and RMB 43,800.

Wen Qing, a pseudonym, is a franchise dealer. He told Time Finance: "We motorcycle dealers knew Zhang Xue's name very early. He is famous in the community—from Huanghe Freedom to co-founding Kove and now creating his own brand, he has always attracted attention. Kove sold well when he was there. ZX Moto's overall specifications now match foreign brands, but its pricing remains at Chinese-brand levels. I thought it was worth doing."

The first batch of 500RRs delivered by ZX Moto. Image source: the company's website.

In September 2024, at the 22nd China International Motorcycle Trade Exhibition, ZX Moto officially unveiled its first model, the four-cylinder 500RR supersport. Wen recalled the strength of Zhang's personal appeal: "The first bike had only been displayed at the show. Soon afterward, the first dealers were already in place, although there were no bikes in stock. I was in the second batch."

After watching for a while, Wen opened his first ZX Moto store in his hometown in April 2025. "We sold twenty in the first month." He admitted he had not expected such strong sales. Only six months later, he opened a second store in another city, where monthly sales soon stabilized at around fifty.

As an experienced motorcycle dealer, Wen sees no obvious market weakness in ZX Moto so far, though building brand awareness still takes time.

"Most buyers are young. Older people trust joint-venture brands between Japanese companies such as Suzuki and Honda and Chinese companies. They think many Chinese brands historically traded low prices for volume and offered worse quality than joint ventures. Young people today are more willing to try new brands and recommend them to friends if they are good."

He believes motorcycles are not fast-moving consumer goods; brand development and growth depend heavily on reputation and product competitiveness. After years of international brands' dominance, ZX Moto still faces challenges in rapidly expanding market share, but its long-term outlook is positive.

He said overall sales are rising rapidly. "Our store's sales increased 20%–30% over these two days. We cannot see other stores' figures, but the ordering system shows that delivery has stretched from one month to two or three."

170 stores in nine months; Europe next this year

Zhang's racing dream has attracted close attention from capital markets.

Tianyancha shows that ZX Moto completed angel financing in July 2024 from Shanghai Gaoxin Lingzhi Venture Capital Partnership (Limited Partnership), a private-equity fund under Gaoxin Capital. The fund has registered capital of RMB 51 million, of which individual limited partner Zhou Jiansu subscribed RMB 50 million.

Zhou is consequently seen as the first patron to recognize Zhang's potential in founding his own brand. Little information about Zhou is public. Tianyancha shows Zhou is also a shareholder of Moxing Yingchuang (Shanghai) Industrial Design Co., Ltd.

Even with investor support, ZX Moto's development has been less smooth than it appears.

Zhang recalled in a media interview that a seed investor contributed RMB 20 million for 20%. He privately borrowed another RMB 10 million from that investor; together with money received on leaving Kove, this gave him over RMB 30 million, intended to last until the first model's March 2025 launch. Instead, money ran short in February, and only by borrowing RMB 7 million from various sources could he stabilize the team.

A stable pool of funds emerged only after the March 2025 launch and start of deliveries, supported by supplier payment terms and dealer deposits.

The latest funding round took place this January.

In January 2026, ZX Moto completed Series A financing led by funds under Zhejiang Venture Capital Group, including Hangzhou Zhechuang Baige Venture Capital Partnership (Limited Partnership) and Jinhua Zhechuang Jinyi Zhikong Venture Capital Partnership (Limited Partnership). It thereby gained state-backed shareholders, and its valuation rose to RMB 1 billion.

As of March 31, Zhang personally held about 73.39% of Chongqing Zhangxue Motorcycle Industry Co., Ltd. Shanghai Gaoxin Lingzhi Venture Capital Partnership and Chongqing Zhangxue Partners Enterprise Management Partnership each held around 9.17%. Jinhua Zhechuang Jinyi Zhikong and Hangzhou Zhechuang Baige held 5.50% and 2.75%, respectively.

With capital behind it, ZX Moto is expanding faster.

On July 1, 2025, it had just 75 operating stores. By March 25, 2026, it had 245 operating stores across 32 provincial-level administrative regions—a net increase of 170 in nine months.

Screenshot from ZX Moto's official WeChat account.

The company continues expanding its dealer network. Its WeChat account lists recruitment areas including numerous lower-tier cities in Yunnan, Sichuan, Shanxi, Anhui, and other provinces. Depending on store categories A through C, franchisees need startup capital of RMB 300,000–1.5 million.

According to CNR, its 2025 output value reached RMB 750 million, R&D investment RMB 69.58 million, and sales more than 25,000 units. In an interview this year, Zhang also disclosed 2025 company sales revenue of RMB 720 million and a loss of RMB 20 million.

His 2026 plan is 450 stores and RMB 2 billion in sales. "At RMB 2 billion we make a profit; RMB 1.5 billion is breakeven; below that we lose money." This implies year-on-year sales growth of 177% within the year.

To meet these growth ambitions, ZX Moto is actively preparing to go overseas. In November last year, it made its global debut at EICMA 2025, presenting a comprehensive motorcycle range tailored to Europe, with plans to enter that market officially in 2026.

Asked what Chinese brands still lack compared with established Japanese and European manufacturers, Zhang identified three areas: brand reputation, development cycles, and engine technology. He acknowledged that reputation takes decades rather than years to build and emphasized longer development cycles to refine products.

He also said he hoped to reach "China's top three within three years and the world's top ten within ten."
