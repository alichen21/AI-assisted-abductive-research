# People of Our Time | Zhang Xue: Do What You Want to Do, and Keep Doing It

> Source: [Xinhua News Agency (Zhou Wenchong and Tian Jinwen)](https://www.news.cn/sports/20260403/8aef7ddf9a9141038e5a56bf86e09395/c.html)

## People of Our Time | Zhang Xue: Do What You Want to Do, and Keep Doing It

With nearly twenty microphones clipped to his collar, Zhang Xue was surrounded by layers of journalists.

A few days earlier, a motorcycle bearing his name won in the Superbike World Championship, leaving many European, American, and Japanese teams exclaiming, "Unbelievable."

Overnight, Zhang reached a wider public. People repeatedly watched a twenty-year-old video of him relentlessly pursuing journalists.

In it, nineteen-year-old Zhang rides more than a hundred kilometers through rain after a television crew, hoping to demonstrate his skills on camera. Perhaps a team would see the broadcast and make his racing dream possible. Falling again and again and getting back up, covered in mud and water, he shouts into the camera: "If you have a dream, pursue it. Courage makes my life more exciting."

Nobody imagined that twenty years later he really would achieve it, albeit in a role other than rider.

Sitting opposite the journalists, Zhang was a little heavier than at nineteen.

After the victory, a friend messaged him: "At last, the clouds have parted and the moon is out." Zhang recalled thinking for a long time before replying seriously: "Actually, I was prepared never to see the moon."

March 30: Zhang surrounded by journalists seeking interviews. Photo by Xinhua reporter Tian Jinwen.

At fourteen, a few months before graduating from junior secondary school, the Huaihua teenager asked his father to buy him several motorcycle-repair books.

He became a repair-shop apprentice and later opened his own shop: "I was with motorcycles every day, learning more about them while supporting myself."

After saving RMB 800, he bought a secondhand off-road motorcycle and practiced hard. A team really did see the television program and took him on as a stunt rider and mechanic.

He began competing. "At first, in off-road races, I just charged ahead without thinking. I really dared to open the throttle," he said. Gradually he mastered starts. Although his technical skill ranked outside his class's top ten, he could take the lead at the start in almost every race.

"I am fairly quick-minded. I can get to the heart of a problem," he said.

Having become a rider, Zhang then changed tracks. In 2009, he went to work at a motorcycle manufacturer in Zhejiang.

Looking back now, no step was wasted.

Repairing motorcycles in Hunan, he dismantled many failed parts, learning which components were unsuitable or prone to breakage. Racing gave him a feel for motorcycles that ordinary engineers lacked. After a few laps, he could give engineers precise feedback. During four years in Zhejiang, he learned the complete manufacturing process and techniques.

In 2013, he arrived in Chongqing with RMB 20,000 to build motorcycles himself.

"Why Chongqing?" a journalist asked.

"The industry cluster," he answered immediately.

At first, he earned money through his skills: posting on forums and selling online, modifying bikes for fellow riders, and handling graphics, customer service, and after-sales work himself. Short of funds, he "crowdfunded," taking payment from riders before buying modification parts.

The hardships of the startup period did nothing to extinguish his passion.

"I set my goals and ideals far away, perhaps very far from where I am," he said. "I leave them there and always move toward them. Every step, even with some twists and turns, must be in that direction."

April 1: Zhang being interviewed. Photo by Xinhua reporter Zhou Wenchong.

In 2017, Zhang co-founded Kove with partners and began formal motorcycle manufacturing. In April 2024, he went independent and founded ZX Moto. That September, the company unveiled its first model at the Chongqing Motorcycle Expo.

The winning model's engine, key components, and complete vehicle setup were all developed domestically.

A rider winning a major race on his brand's motorcycle is the best advertisement. On race night, Zhang gathered with nearly a thousand enthusiasts on open ground in Chongqing to watch the live broadcast. When the bike crossed the line, the place erupted. His tears flowed freely—a moment perhaps understood only by those who have failed.

Annual competition costs are RMB 10–20 million, potentially requiring sales of 10,000 bikes. "I did not know we would definitely win. I did not even know if I could earn back all that money," he said. "Why do we come into this world? Is it not to experience life? Failing would be an experience too, but if I never tried, there would be no experience."

He had expected a podium this year and a race win next year; the victory unexpectedly "disrupted his plans." Orders doubled within days, and he had to raise production capacity.

"The bottleneck is R&D," he said with some urgency. The company has more than 200 engineers and can advance only three or four projects simultaneously. "With 500 engineers, we could launch more models, faster." He works more than ten hours daily, focusing most of his effort on R&D, and can rush straight to the workshop when problems arise.

Yin Zhongyi, an employee who has followed Zhang for seven years, said he once feared the company might collapse in its hardest period. Those worries have vanished. Recently, many enthusiasts have come to photograph the factory, and many motorcycle-loving young people ask: "How can I work here?"

ZX Moto's recruitment rules give applicants who can ride motorcycles five extra interview points. Zhang hopes candidates come with the same passion as his own, seeing the place as more than just a job.

April 1: Workers inspect completed motorcycles before dispatch at ZX Moto's Chongqing workshop. Photo by Xinhua reporter Zhou Wenchong.

After he reached a wider audience, a journalist asked whether hard work guarantees success. "If you truly like and want something, and work hard enough, you can make it happen. How could you not?" His words resonated online and were widely liked and shared.

Even now, he often recalls chasing the journalists on his motorcycle twenty years ago.

"If you could go back twenty years, what would you say to yourself?" a journalist asked.

He thought, then said: "Do what you want to do, and keep doing it. That is the right way." (Reporters: Zhou Wenchong and Tian Jinwen)
