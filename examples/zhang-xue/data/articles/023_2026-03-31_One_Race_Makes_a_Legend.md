# A Single Race Makes a Legend! There Is Light in His Eyes!

> Source: [CCTV News](https://people.cctv.com/2026/03/31/ARTI5NM8NqZfD939kXmb3nsA260331.shtml)

## A Single Race Makes a Legend! He Made the World Remember Chinese Motorcycles

Recently, the atmosphere at Portugal's Portimão circuit during the Superbike World Championship (WSBK) was electric! A French rider on a machine built by Chinese manufacturer ZX Moto won both Race 1 and Race 2 in the SSP class, the middleweight category, securing back-to-back victories!

WSBK is a stage for testing the ultimate performance of production motorcycles. This Chinese manufacturer's victory broke the long-standing dominance of European, American, and Japanese brands such as Ducati and Yamaha.

In an exclusive interview with a China Media Group reporter, founder Zhang Xue could barely contain his excitement. Years of persistence and effort had finally borne fruit. Asked what made the victory possible, he said the team's motorcycle had core advantages in its displacement class: the most horsepower, the lowest weight, and the lowest chassis center of gravity.

An electrifying race

Chinese motorcycle becomes a double winner

On March 28, Beijing time, in Race 1 of the SSP class at WSBK's Portuguese round, a French rider took victory on ZX Moto's 820RR-RS with a lead of nearly four seconds over second place. Internet users called the scene "a masterpiece."

In Race 2 the next day, March 29, ZX Moto won again, securing two consecutive victories. The team's motorcycle was particularly strong in straight-line acceleration.

△ Brand founder Zhang Xue celebrates the team's victory.

Founded in Chongqing and named after its founder, the brand reached the world's summit in two years, completing a stunning rise that resonated across Chinese manufacturing. Zhang cried with excitement while watching the live broadcast. He later wrote on social media: "When you do something out of love rather than chasing an outcome, perhaps the result really will be different."

Why name the brand after himself? Zhang told the CMG reporter: "As long as my products are good enough and can win every competition, the 'name' may become a top-tier symbol."

Saving up for a battered secondhand motorcycle

was his first step toward his dream

Born in rural Huaihua, Hunan, Zhang encountered motorcycle repair as a child and developed a deep interest in motorcycles.

At fourteen, carrying a worn backpack, he walked into a motorcycle repair shop in Huaihua and became an apprentice.

He got up before dawn to open the shop, sweep the yard, clean tools, and dismantle and wash oil-covered engines. He worked more than ten hours a day. Black engine oil never quite washed out from under his fingernails, and just-healed cuts were reopened by parts. What others saw as dirty, exhausting labor was, to him, a ladder toward his dream. He studied dismantled parts late into the night.

Living frugally, he saved enough to buy a secondhand motorcycle older than himself and riddled with problems. It became the starting point for his racing dream. He repeatedly practiced stunts on rural dirt roads, falling until he was covered in bruises. When the bike broke, he repaired it himself and practiced again.

To make himself seen,

he rode more than 100 kilometers in the rain

In 2006, nineteen-year-old Zhang did something others considered almost "crazy": he rode more than 100 kilometers in the rain simply to get a chance to demonstrate his skills on camera.

Eager to join a motorcycle team, he repeatedly contacted a television crew to ask to show his riding skills. But when filming began, he failed. He refused to give up, following the crew through the rain until his persistence moved them to give him a second filming opportunity.

Nineteen-year-old Zhang left viewers with two lines: "If you have a dream, pursue it. Courage makes my life more exciting."

The footage of him racing through the rain introduced countless people to this stubborn young man from western Hunan. His sincerity and persistence prompted a professional team to offer him a place, finally opening the door to professional motorcycling.

After joining, he became a stunt rider and mechanic.

He finished third in the domestic class of the 2009 urban motorcycle stair-climbing competition and second in the same event in 2011, proving himself repeatedly. But practical difficulties followed: injuries from years of intensive training and shortcomings in China's motorcycle industry. At that time, China still could not build a truly outstanding large-displacement racing motorcycle, and foreign companies had long monopolized core technologies.

Gradually, Zhang realized he might never reach the summit as a rider, but could change tracks and bring Chinese motorcycles to the forefront. "If I cannot ride at the front, I will build a bike that lets Chinese people get there."

Without an independently developed engine,

you will always be dependent on foreign brands

From 2009 to 2012, Zhang worked with motorcycles at a Zhejiang factory, mastering the whole vehicle-development process. In 2013, he went to Chongqing, a major center of China's motorcycle industry, and began his own motorcycle-building journey on this fertile ground for such dreams.

There, he built his first truly "Zhang Xue-made" motorcycle. It was China's first fuel-injected motorcycle priced below RMB 10,000. He wanted more ordinary people to ride dependable motorcycles with good performance.

He had now made the crucial transition from repairing and modifying motorcycles to building them. But he kept going: without independently developed engines, a company would remain an "assembly plant," forever vulnerable to foreign brands' control.

In 2017, thirty-year-old Zhang and several partners founded Kove. Its first model became an immediate hit, with dealers lining up to collect stock. Everyone urged him to capitalize on the momentum by making more high-volume, profitable models.

Yet he decided once again to pursue independent development of large-displacement engines. He poured his entire fortune into R&D, leading a team of a dozen or so people who ate and slept at the factory, testing, rejecting, and starting again day and night through countless failures and fresh starts.

He told the CMG reporter: "I have a high tolerance for failure, so I never fear it. When an idea comes, I act immediately. Even if I get it wrong, I adjust promptly. I do not worry about saving face; when I am wrong, I face the mistake."

Pursuing his "stars and seas"

until reaching the top of the Superbike World Championship

In 2024, Zhang decided to leave the company he had helped found and start again. He remained determined to tackle the difficult task he had always wanted to pursue: engine development.

△ Zhang's resignation letter: "I have decided to resign to pursue my stars and seas."

In April 2024, he founded ZX Moto, named after himself, in Chongqing's Liangjiang New Area.

△ At the Chongqing venue of CMG's 2025 Year of the Snake Spring Festival Gala, ZX Moto motorcycles cross Qiansimen Bridge.

In 2026, ZX Moto's three-cylinder 820RR supersport went on sale. This road-going production motorcycle, modified for the track, went straight into the Superbike World Championship. With an independently developed Chinese engine, key components, and vehicle setup, it claimed two wins.

The boy whose hands were covered in oil in a repair shop at fourteen finally made the world see Chinese motorcycles through a machine he built himself.
